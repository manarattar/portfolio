% ============ THESIS ============
\section*{Master's Thesis}
\project{Author Profiling of Hate Speech Spreaders: Zero-shot LLMs vs.\ Fine-tuned Encoders}{MA Thesis, 2026}
\begin{tights}
  \item Investigated whether the age and gender of hate speech authors can be inferred from their writing, comparing two competing paradigms under identical conditions: \textbf{zero-shot large language models} (LLaMA-3.1-8B, Qwen3-32B) prompted without task-specific training, and \textbf{encoder models fine-tuned} on labelled data (BERT, HateBERT, RoBERTa), with an \textbf{SVM} lexical baseline for reference.
  \item Designed a cross-domain evaluation --- models trained on \textbf{PAN14} social media data and tested on \textbf{LiLaH-HAG} hate speech comments --- to measure how well author profiling transfers into an abusive-language domain it was not built for.
  \item Found that \textbf{neither approach reached performance sufficient to recommend it} for deployment: zero-shot LLMs held a narrow edge on gender and fine-tuned encoders on age, but all results sat close to the majority baseline, so the comparison is inconclusive by design rather than by omission.
  \item Argued from confusion-matrix and lexical error analysis that the ceiling is a property of the domain, not the models: hate speech is a strongly conventionalised register in which shared genre norms suppress the individual stylistic signal author profiling depends on.
  \item Extended the study to \textbf{Slovene} (CroSloEngual BERT) to test whether grammatical-gender marking supplies a signal English lacks --- the strongest result in the study, and evidence that language-specific cues matter more than model scale.
\end{tights}
